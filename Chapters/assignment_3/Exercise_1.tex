\section*{Exercise 9.1}

Consider full conformal prediction with an exchangeable dataset and a
symmetric score function. Instead of comparing the test score against
all $n$ training scores, compare it against a random subset indexed by
$I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, sampled independently of the data:
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})
    =\left\{y\in\mathcal{Y}:S_{n+1}^{y}\leqslant
    \operatorname{Quantile}\left((S_i^{y})_{i\in I};
    (1-\alpha)\left(1+\frac{1}{|I|}\right)\right)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Prove that this modified prediction set has marginal coverage at least
$1-\alpha$.

\paragraph{Solution}
Let $Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)$, $D_{n+1}=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{n+1})$, and define the
scores at the true test response by
\begin{equation*}
    S_i=s(Z_i;D_{n+1}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n+1.
\end{equation*}
Symmetry of $s$ and exchangeability of the data imply that
$(S_1,\ldots,S_{n+1})$ is exchangeable. Indeed, for every permutation
$\pi$ of the $n+1$ indices,
\begin{equation*}
    (S_{\pi(i)})_{i=1}^{n+1}
    =\bigl(s(Z_{\pi(i)};(D_{n+1})_{\pi})\bigr)_{i=1}^{n+1}
    \overset{d}{=}(S_i)_{i=1}^{n+1}.
\end{equation*}
The equality uses symmetry in the dataset argument, and the equality in
distribution uses exchangeability of the data.

Now condition on $I=J$, for any fixed nonempty subset $J$ having positive
probability, and put $m=|J|$. Independence of $I$ and the data means that
conditioning on $I=J$ does not change the distribution of the scores.
Thus the $m$ selected scores together with $S_{n+1}$ remain exchangeable.
Let
\begin{equation*}
    k_m=\lceil(m+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil.
\end{equation*}
If $k_m\leqslant m$, the quantile in the question is the $k_m$th smallest
score among $(S_i)_{i\in J}$. The exchangeable-rank argument gives
\begin{equation*}
    P\{Y_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})\mid I=J\}
    \geqslant\frac{k_m}{m+1}\geqslant1-\alpha.
\end{equation*}
For completeness, break any score ties uniformly at random solely for
this rank argument. The rank of $S_{n+1}$ among the $m+1$ scores is then
uniform on $\{1,\ldots,m+1\}$. Rank at most $k_m$ implies that
$S_{n+1}$ is no larger than the selected-sample quantile, which proves
the first inequality even when scores tie.
If $k_m=m+1$, the quantile is $+\infty$ by the conformal convention,
and the conditional coverage is $1$.

Averaging the conditional guarantees over all possible subsets yields
\begin{equation*}
    \boxed{P\{Y_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})\}
    =\sum_J P(I=J)
    P\{Y_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})\mid I=J\}
    \geqslant1-\alpha.}
\end{equation*}
If an empty subset is allowed, define its threshold to be $+\infty$ and
return $\mathcal{Y}$ on that event; the same conclusion follows.
Notice that $I$ need not be uniform among subsets, nor have a fixed size.
