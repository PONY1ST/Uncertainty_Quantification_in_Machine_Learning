\section*{Exercise 1}

Given exchangeable data set $(X_1, Y_1), \cdots, (X_{n}, Y_{n}), (X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1})
\in \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y}$,
level $\alpha \in (0,1)$,
and a real valued symmetric score function $s((x,y); \mathcal{D})$
for $(x,y) \in \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y}$
and $\mathcal{D} \in (\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y})^k$
for any $k$ being a data set,
we carry out the full conformal prediction.
Put, for any $y \in \mathcal{Y}$:

\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{D}_{n+1}^y = \curly{(X_1, Y_1), \cdots, (X_{n}, Y_{n}), (X_{n+1}, y)}.
\end{equation*}
For simplicity, write $S_i^y = s((X_i, Y_i); \mathcal{D}_{n+1}^y)$
for $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$
and $S_{n+1}^y = s((X_{n+1}, y); \mathcal{D}_{n+1}^y)$.
The standard conformal prediction procedure 
asks us to compare $S_{n+1}^y$
with the entire sequence $(S_{1}^y, \ldots, S_n^y)$.

We proceed however by choosing randomly 
a subset $I \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}$ 
independent of the data 
and compare $S_{n+1}^y$ with $(S_i^y)_{i \in I}$.
More precisely, put:

\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})
    =\curly{y \in \mathcal{Y} \mid S_{n+1}^{y} \leqslant
    \text{Quantile} \bracket{(S_i^{y})_{i \in I};
    (1-\alpha)\bracket{1+\frac{1}{|I|} } }}.
\end{equation*}
Prove that 

\begin{equation*}
    P \curly{Y_{n+1} \in \mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})} \geqslant 1 - \alpha.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Solution}
Given that $I$ is independent of the data set,
the joint distribution of 
$((X_1, Y_1), \cdots, (X_{n}, Y_{n}), (X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}))$
remains the same after conditioning on $I$.
In particular, they are still exchangeable.
Therefore:

\begin{equation*}
    P \curly{Y_{n+1} \in \mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})} = \sum P\{I = J\} P\bracket{
        \curly{Y_{n+1} \in \mathcal{C}(X_{n+1})} \mid I = J} \geqslant \sum P\{I = J\} (1 - \alpha) = 1 - \alpha,
\end{equation*}
where the summation is taken with $J$ ranged over all subsets of ${1, \ldots, n}$.
The second inequality is Theorem 3.2 in the textbook.
